### First Political Parties:
- After/During GW but GW wasn't a part of either
- Hamiltonian **Federalists**:
	- Strong Central Govt.
	- Loose interpretation of Constitution
	- Encourage commerce and manufacturing economy
	- Close ties with GB, wary of French revolution
	- Emphasized order
- Jeffersonian **Republicans**
	- State power
	- Strict interpretation of Constitution
	- Agricultural based economy -> "Yeoman farmer republic"
	- favored the French, saw revolution as extension of American Rev.

### One Party, Several Factions (Era of good feelings):
- **Clay** Faction:
	- Pro-Natl Bank
	- Pro-Tariff
	- Pro-Infrastructure
	- Unhappy with Jackson's veto
- **Jackson** Faction:
	- Strongly against Natl Bank
	- Not a fan of tariffs but enforced them
	- Didn't like either Clay or Calhoun
- **Calhoun** Faction:
	- Pro-Slavery
	- Vehemently against tariffs and infrastructure building
	- Unhappy with Jackson threatening force on tariff issue

### Second 2-Party System:
- Largely split after 
- **Whigs** (Henry Clay):
	- For Clay's American System
	- Pro Natl Bank
	- Favored Industry -> Free/Wage labor, not slavery
	- Pro expansion, but sometimes against Mexican American War
- **Democrats** (Van Buren/Polk):
	- Against Clay's American System
	- Pro Slave Labor
	- For Aggressive expansion
	- Opposed state legislative reforms -> preferred individual freedom of choice

### New Parties and Realignment (1850s):
- **Free Soil Party**:
	- Opposed expansion of slavery into new territories
	- will make up an influential faction in the republican party
- **American party/Know nothings**:
	- Nativist party -> against catholic immigration
- **Republican Party (Party of Lincoln)**:
	- Made up of Free Soil, Conscience Whigs, and Know Nothings 
	- For paid labor
	- Opposed expansion of Slavery into new territories
	- Some opposed slavery as morally wrong, while still affirming Southern States right to have it
	- Regionally based in North and West Coast
- **Democratic Party**:
	- For greater protections of slavery
	- For expansion of slavery into new territories
	- Regionally based in South

### Radical Republican Era (1861-1870s):
- Republicans control congress (Democrats left b/c of civil war)
- **Many important reforms**:
	- Homestead Act
	- Transcontinental RR subsidies
	- Natl Banking Act
	- 13th, 14th, 15th Amendments
	- Several Civil Rights Acts to support the amendments
	- Freedmen's Bureau
- **1877 ended Reconstruction and the Radical Era** -> North Pulled Military support from the South

### Gilded Age Political Parties (Late 1870s-1900):
- **Republicans and Democrats**:
	- Closely fought elections
	- **Both parties were very similar**
		- Both pro business
		- Anti Unions
		- Laissez faire
		- Support Gold Standard
		- Resisted Radical Reforms
- **Populist Party**:
	- Granger and Farmers' Alliance movements form political party
	- Supported:
		- Tariff reduction
		- Graduated Income Tax
		- Govt. control of RRs
		- Popular vote of Senators
		- Free Silver

### Progressive Era (1900-1920):
- **Teddy Roosevelt, Taft, Woodrow Wilson**
- **Republicans and Democrats**:
	- Progressive and conservative factions in both major parties
	- Progressives in Congress and Presidents transform govt:
		- Viewed Govt as important for creating orderly and sable society
		- believed that Govt reform can help fix corruption
		- Govt interference in economy was valuable for reinvigorating capitalism

### Conservatism Returns (1920s):
- **Conservatism**:
	- Republicans dominated national politics throughout the 1920s
	- Democrats only strong in south and some big cities
	- **Return to business-friendly congress**:
		- Slight return to Laissez Faire
	- **Social Tensions dominated politics**:
		- Anti-Immigration
		- Prohibition
		- Race Relations

### FDR, New Deal, and WWII (1933-1945):
- Democrats and FDR sweep legislative and executive branches.
- New deal programs aim to provide relief to citizens, reform economy, and recover from GD
- FDR power grows as he breaks 2 term tradition and directs the war effort
- Republicans have limited ability to protest against the government movements.

### Post WWII Realignment (1945-1968):
- Dwight Eisenhower, JFK, Nixon, LBJ
- **Democrats**:
	- Continued supporting New Deal reforms
	- LBJ's Great Society programs expanded govt and welfare
	- Aligned Democrats with Civil Rights movement
- **Republicans**:
	- Moderate Reps (**Eisenhower**):
		- wanted more business friendly govt
		- didn't want to get rid of New Deal reforms
	- Conservative Reps (**Goldwater**):
		- wanted a drastic reduction in size of govt and taxes 
		- Pro-States' Rights
	- S'n Democrats switch to republican party

### Nixon-land and Malaise (1966-1980):
- **Democrats**:
	- Party is fragmented due to Vietnam War
	- Dems take permanent liberal turn in 1970s -> push fro civil rights, reproductive rights, fair housing, etc.
	- Stagflation, rising crime rates,
- **Republicans**:
	- Nixon is able to position himself between moderates and conservative republicans:
	- cut back on govt, opposed social problems
	- Nixon's Silent Majority -> patriotic, family oriented, conservative
	- Watergate Scandal damages republicans
	- Reagan emerges

### Rise of Conservatism and Deep Polarization:
- **Democrats**:
	- Democrats shift to middle
	- Pres. Clinton works with Republicans to curb welfare
	- Continues tough on crime stance
	- 2000s:
		- Opposition to Pres. Bush and Iraq war
		- Push for more active govt on healthcare
	- 2010s:
		- Obama attempts to reform healthcare
		- increase govt. regulations
- **Republicans:**
	- 1980s:
		- Social conservatives
		- Economic Conservatives
		- Neoconservatives
	- 1990s and 2000s:
		- Republicans rinse and try to repeat Reagan coalition
		- Push back against left's call for social changes
	- 2010:
		- Conservatives both elite and grassroots vary locally (TEA party protests)
		- oppose Obama's reforms
