## The Transportation Revolution Forges Regional Ties
#### Canals and Steamboats Shrink Distance –
- State governments paid private companies to build toll roads (“turnpikes”);
	- 1806: Congress appropriates money for a **National Road** of compacted gravel
		- Spans from Maryland (1811) to Illinois by 1839
- Land Travel slow, *states increase water travel*
	- *Erie Canal* - connected Hudson River (Albany, NY) to Lake Erie
		- Erie’s huge success encouraged building of other canals; <u>by 1848 could travel all-water route from NYC to New Orleans</u>
	- Steamboats used for travel & transport
#### Wilderness Road
- Main route used by settlers for 50 yrs to reach Kentucky from East
	- 1775, Daniel Boone starts trail for Transylvania Co.
- Ft. Chiswell (VA) thru Cumberland Gap into Central KY
	- 1796, better, all-weather road opened for wagon and carriage travel
#### Railroads Foster Regional Ties-
- Canals, roads, steamboats, railroads -> improved transportation -> larger markets + new lands for settlement
- Transportation *promoted diversified economies* in Northeast + Midwest, <u>NOT in South</U>
- NY, Boston, and London capitalists invested in the railroad industry; a boom in the *1850s* expanded commerce;
- Ability to transport good produced in Midwest via railroad -> Chicago growth
- Midwestern farmers able to export crops to East + Europe
	- John Deere's farming equipment manufacturer grew in region
- *Northeast + Midwest diversify economies, South remains tied to agriculture*
## The Growth of Cities and Towns:
#### West and Midwest -
- urban pop. in U.S. *grew substantially*
	- Specifically in West + *Midwest*, + around <u>"fall line"</u> (where diff. elevation regions meet)
	- towns grew around factories
- cities that started as locations of commerce eventually grew to be:
	- *manufacturing centers* (Chicago + St. Louis)
	- *transit centers* (Pittsburgh, Cincinnati, New Orleans)
#### Atlantic Coastal Cities - 
- Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Baltimore remained important for import/export
	- also became financial centers
- *immigration* to port cities -> pop. grew
- NY hub for exporting cargo, mail, + people to Liverpool & London
	- NYC dominates foreign + domestic trade w/ harbor and Erie Canal
## APUSH Review: Video #21: The Market Revolution
#### What is the Market Revolution?
- **Revolution in transportation, farming, & production of goods**
	- increased use of canals, roads, steamboats, and railroads
	- subsistence farming -> cash-crop farming
	- goods increasing produced *outside* the home
- PRIOR to Civil War (***Antebellum America***)
#### Transportation Innovations:
- Railroads - Cumberland (National) Road
- Canals - Erie
- Steamboats - Robert Fulton
#### Agricultural Improvements:
- Cotton Gin (Eli Whitney)
	- separated cotton from seeds as faster rate
	- Whitney believed it would decrease slavery
		- Actually increased it, cotton boom?
			- cotton more valuable
- Steel Plow (benefitted Midwest)
- McCormick Reaper - increase harvesting efficiency
#### Technological Innovations:
- Interchangeable parts - Eli Whitney
	- Made equipment easier to repair
- *Telegraph* - increased communication
	- game changer, can sent messages far easily now
#### State and Federal Improvements? (2)
- State/Federal govs. often funded roads, canals, & RRs
	- Erie Canal - paid by NYS (Intrastate trade)
	- Cumberland Rd - paid by Federal gov. (interstate trade)

#### Regional Links and Products?
- Closely Linked?
	- North + Midwest often linked together
		- Benefit more than South, more infrastructure
	- Regions become more reliant on each other, trade more
- Midwest Products
	- pigs, corn, cattle
- South Products
	- cotton
- North Products
	- manufactured goods
#### Emergence of Corporations
- able to buy stock
	- become part owner, but aren't liable
#### Factories and Society-
- Young children work in factories
- Poor families move from farming -> factory work
- Men/Women
	- work outside home in larger numbers
- Lowell Factory, Massachusetts
	- Farmers' daughters work in factory
		- repetitive & simple work, textile factory
		- Late teens - early 20s (single women)
	- Worked for short periods, save $
	- Women lived in boarding houses maintained by owners
		- had curfew, made to attend church
#### Impacts of the Market Revolution?
- Growing middle class
- Increase in prosperity and standard of living for a portion of the population
- *Emergence of business elite*
	- Factory owners become rich
	- Unequal distribution of wealth
#### *Cult of Domesticity*

	- raise children to instill American values in children (similar to *Republican Motherhood*)?
