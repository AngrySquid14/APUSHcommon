## Federalists
- Upper class, merchants, Pro-British (trade!), <u>located mostly in the North</u>
## Democratic-Republicans:
- Middle and lower classes, **farmers**, Pro-French (American Revolution), located in the South
## Revolution of 1800?
- **Peaceful transition of power** between Federalists to Democratic-Republicans
	- Election of 1800, Thomas Jefferson (Democratic Republican) wins
## Louisiana Purchase?
- Mississippi River was important (both for merchants & farmers)
- **Doubled** the size of the U.S.
- <u>Cause of Jefferson switching from strict to loose interpretation of the Constitution</u>
- Jefferson liked idea of gaining lots of farm land
## Causes of the War of 1812?
- British *impressment* 
	- taking captured sailors and forcing them to fight
- Arming of Native Americans by the British, War Hawks (*Henry Clay*)
	- War Hawks = those eager for war w/ Britain/ war in general?
## Impact of the War of 1812?
- **Andrew Jackson** & William Henry Harrison become war heroes
- increase in **NATIONALISM**
## Hartford Convention
- Federalists wanted to amend Constitution
- Some urged *secession*
- **Led to the end of the federalists**
	- *Note*: Monroe era also marked end of federalists
	- Why? America was experiencing nationalism, Federalists appear selfish
## Supreme Court Decisions
#### Marbury v. Madison
- **ESTABLISHED JUDICIAL REVIEW**
	- right of Supreme Court to review government actions and decide if they are *Constitutional*
#### McCulloch v. Maryland
- Upheld the Constitutionality of the BUS
- Federal power > state power
#### Gibbons v. Ogden
- Congress had sole authority over **Inter**state trade
#### Marshall Court-Results
- Increased the power of the federal government
- Ruled in favor of the federal government > state government
- Improved economy (Gibbons-state trade, McCulloch, BUS, etc.)
## Slavery
#### Passive ways of resisting slavery
- Faking illness, working slowly, breaking tools, etc.
#### Violent Rebellions:
- Nat Turner's rebellion (1831)
	- Largest slave rebellion in history?
	- Like all slave rebellions, <u>caused harsher slave codes and worse treatment</u>
#### Abolitionist writings?
- David Walker’s Appeal to the Colored Citizens of the World
	- Urged *violent resistance*
#### William Lloyd Garrison’s *The Liberator*
- Advocated the *immediate* and *uncompensated* end to slavery
	- “I will be as harsh as truth, and uncompromising as justice... I am in earnest, I will not equivocate, I will not excuse, I will not retreat a single inch, and I will be heard.”
#### How did the South respond to calls for abolitionism?
- They defended slavery as a “*positive good*”
### Market Revolution
#### What was it?
- *major Improvements in manufacturing and transportation*
#### Technology improvements:
-  Interchangeable Parts
- Eli Whitney, <u>increased production, decreased prices</u>
- <u>Steam Engine</u>
- Robert Fulton, boats can go against the current
#### Agricultural inventions
- John Deere’s steal plow – breaking soil in the *Midwest*
- McCormick Reaper – increase in crop cultivation
#### American System, 3 parts
- *Thought up by Henry Clay*
	- Bank of the US (2nd)
	- <u>Internal Improvements</u> (canals, roads, infrastructure)
	- Tariffs (1816 – protective tariffs)
#### What two areas benefitted the MOST from the Market Revolution?
- **North and Midwest**
### Section Tensions
#### 3 parts of the Missouri Compromise, Go!
- Maine created as a free state
- Missouri gets to be added to union as a slave state
- 36°30’ line: above is free, below is slave
#### Impact of the Missouri Compromise?
- Short term success, long-term failure
- Lasted ~34 years
#### Nullification Crisis:
- Over tariffs, *South disliked*, why?
	- Higher prices for goods, they received no tariff protection
#### South Carolina Exposition and Protest
- Written by John C. Calhoun (*anonymously*)
- Encouraged SC and other states to *nullify* the *Tariff of "abominations"*
	- AKA Tariff of 1828 - designed to not pass congress, still did
		- *Extremely high* protective tariff
- Drew its inspiration from the <u>VA and KY Resolutions</u>
	- which were against Alien & Sedition acts - reinforced right of state to *nullify* acts
