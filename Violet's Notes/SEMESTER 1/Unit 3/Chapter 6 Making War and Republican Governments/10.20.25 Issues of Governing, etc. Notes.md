### Issues of Governing?
- Power vs. Liberty?
	- Too much power = Tyranny
	- Too much liberty = Anarchy
- *Many colonial leaders were anti-democracy*
	- Why? Wealthy, don't want to lose power
	- Think there's too much power with the "people"
### Articles of Confederation
<u> Created central government w/ limited power, in order to avoid British government. Issues eventually led to calls for more powerful government </u>
- *Written in 1777 by John Dickinson* (Penn. statesman)
- Accepted by Congress in 1781, considered **first national constitution**
- Requires all 13 states to agree to change federal law (difficult)
	- Rhode Island - usually votes NO on principle
- Fear of having too much power in one person's hand reflects experiences of colonies under monarchy
- Articles- state governments limit power of national Congress
#### Results: Had many weaknesses, causing...
	- Government short on money
	- States fighting, hard to trade w/ other countries
	- Gov. depended on states for law enforcement
	- Difficult to enact laws
	- No practical way to change powers of gov.
	- No effective wat to coordinate work of government
	- Central government had no way to settle disputes among states
##### Weaknesses of the Articles of Confederation (cont.)
- Congress had no power to raise taxes, regulate foreign & state trade, and to enforce laws
- Laws had to be approved by 9/13 states
###### Problems Under the Articles
- Trade: each state could place tariffs on goods from other states - discouraged trade between states
- *Finances: Each state able to coin its own $*
	- Results: differing values, high inflation in some states, discourage trade
	- Increased taxes - states had debt from Rev. War
	- Fed. Gov. couldn't require taxes
- Foreign Relations:
	- **Britain - Refuse commercial treaties w/ US**, Congress couldn't control commerce (sanctions against Britain)
#### Strengths of the Articles
- <u>Treaty of Paris (1783) Signed</u>
- <u>Northwest Ordinance passed</u>
- Had power to declare war & peace, print money, make treaties, & settle state disputes
- *Settling Western Lands*
	- **The Land Ordinance of 1785** - stated that land in the west was to be surveyed w/ grid system, establishing 6 mile blocks
	- **The Northwest Ordinance** assisted in orderly expansion of the United States, outlining a plan for applying for statehood to western territories
		- 5k *free males who own 50 acres* (1 acre = a football field) can start govt
		- Population of *60,000* could become a state
		- Promised education, freedom of religion, trial by jury, and no slavery
###### Northwest Territory
- East of Mississippi River, north of Ohio River
	- *States Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, & Wisconsin would be formed from this area.*
