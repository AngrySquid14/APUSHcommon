## The Draft/Conscription
- ### Confederate Draft
	- *Originally imposed by Confederacy*
	- Required existing soldiers to serve for duration of the war
	- Mandated 4 years of military service for all white men ages 18-35
		- September 1862 -> Age limit raised to 45 after casualties at Antietam
	- **Two Major Loopholes in the Southern Draft**: was controversial
		- <u>One White man exempted per 20 slaves</u> 
			- => some large plantation whites avoided military service
		- <u>Draftees could hire substitutes</u>
			- => by the time loophole was closed in 1864, price of substitute was $300 in gold, *3 times the annual wage of a skilled worker*
				- Caused complaints from laborers and yeomen, calling it "a rich man's war and a poor man's fight"
	- Some southerners refused to serve
		- Confederate constitution vested sovereignty in individual states -> Richmond government couldn't compel military service
		- Governors from Georgia and NC ignored draft
- ### Union Draft
	- More strict on enforcing draft and acting towards Confederate sympathizers
		- Levied taxes on Southern supporters
		- *Lincoln suspends Habeas Corpus* to temp. imprison 15k Southern sympathizers w/o trial
	- Incentivized men to join war with cash bounties (??) 
	- Also allowed draftees to provide substitute or pay $300 fee
	- *Opposition to Union Draft*
		- New German and Irish immigrants refused to serve, arguing it was not their war.
		- Northern Democrats accused Lincoln of sacrificing poor whites to liberate enslaved blacks
			- Criticized b/c they believed free blacks would flood cities + take jobs from whites

## Suspension of *Habeas Corpus*
- **Habeas Corpus**: right for any person under arrest to appear before the court, to ensure they were not falsely accused
	- Protected by constitution, *can not be suspended* UNLESS in cases of rebellion, invasion, or "when the public safety may require it"
- President Lincoln suspends habeas corpus, citing rebellion
	- Uses its suspension to arrest rioters in Maryland (1861)
	- Prevents Confederate troops to move on D.C.
	- *Controversial decision*, makes Lincoln less popular
