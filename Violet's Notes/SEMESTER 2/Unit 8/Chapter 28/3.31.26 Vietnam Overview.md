### The Sides
- *U.S.* and **The Southern Republic of Vietnam** vs. Southern Revolutionaries (**Viet Cong**) and their *North Vietnamese* sponsor
## Previous Foreign Power Controlling Vietnam
- <u>France</u> occupied Indochina (Southeast Asia) during colonial period
	- Cambodia, Laos, Vietnam
## WWII Foreign Power
- During WWII, Japan gains Vietnam
## Vietnam Independence Post-WWII
- 1945, End of WWII -> Vietnam's Communist leader, *Ho Chi Minh* resists Japan -> Vietnam independent
- **U.S. fears Communist power in Vietnam => Huge financial support in *returning France to rule*** 
	- -> Fights break out against French (1947), U.S. backs French-sponsored leader, *Bao Dai*
	- By 1953, U.S. financing 80% of "France's" war costs
- *1954* -> French defeated in battle at *Dien Bien Phu*, give up rule in Vietnam
## Geneva Convention
- *Geneva International Peace conference* -> *Ho Chi Minh* pressured to accept <u>temporary division of North & South Vietnam</U> at the 17th Parallel
	- Division was until elections would be held (2 years) to decide Vietnam's govt.
- + Cambodia & Laos granted independence from France
- New South leader, *Ngo Dinh Diem* (Catholic) sponsored by U.S.
	- Signed agreement w/ Ho Chi Minh
## Ngo Dinh Diem's Decline
- U.S. provides financial & military aid, hoping to prevent Communist North's election win
	- U.S. supports Diem's <u>refusal to hold free elections</u>
- Diem's popularity declines
	- Diem Catholic, majority Vietnamese Buddhist
	- City folk gain economically, rural people suffer
	- => Increase in Viet Cong supporters
- ### The End of the Southern Leader
	- *1963* -> South, even with superior technology, loses battle at Ap Bac
	- Buddhists Monks protest Catholic leader & religious persecution
		- Some self-immolate (torching)
	- *November 1, 1963* -> Fear of losing war => U.S./Kennedy(?) supports overthrowing of Diem
## Gulf of Tonkin Incident
- *August 2, 1964* -> 2 U.S. Destroyers unharmed, claim being attacked by North Vietnamese
	- => *Gulf of Tonkin Resolution* -> Congress gave Presidential power to <U>"take all necessary measures to repel any armed attack [against U.S. forces] and to prevent further aggression"</u> (against N. VN)
		- => *full-scale war* & draft
## North Vietnam & South-Viet Cong Supporter Strategies
- Many Southern peasants either neutral or supportive of North
- Viet Cong *weak in air power*, find other ways to fight
	- Make up for weakness by fighting through mass network of tunnels
	- Able to retreat to Cambodia when threatened
	- Use variety of booby traps & mines, hard to detect in jungle region
	- Guerilla tactics -> Ambush & sabotage U.S. bases
## U.S. Strategies and Responses to Them
- U.S. strategy was to use heavy fire power, and to *clear the jungle out*
- Use *agent orange* & other chemicals => later stop *due to health concerns*, and creation of many *refugees* from destroyed homes
- Americans left South Vietnamese soldiers to battle cities & rural people
	- S. VN increasingly against war cause

## Tet Offensive (Turning Point)
- *January 1968* -> During lunar new year (Tet) holiday, N. VN & VC forces launch coordinated attacks on 100+ S. VN locations
	- News coverage shocks American public, *eroding support for the war effort* => Americans begin to view it as <u>an unwinnable war</u>
## My Lai Massacre & Anti-War Movement
- ### Anti-War Movement
	- Grew as American casualties rose to 1k deaths per month
	- Media coverage negative, brought battlefield scenes to American public
	- Citizens often upset either because U.S. wasn't winning war, or out of fear of being drafted
- ### My Lai Massacre (1970)
	- American troops try to root out Southern infiltrators, instead kill *300 civilians*
		- => Anti-War sentiment increases from 50% to 2-1 ratio
## Vietnamization
 - Policy by Nixon (starting in 1968) that responded to anti-war movement
	 - Police reduced U.S. troops, put more of war burden on S. VN
 - Draft age lowered in S. VN to *18* as to increase # of troops
	 - => Widespread protests in S. VN, inflation rose, govt. became split
 - *1970* -> Coup topples *neutral* Cambodian govt. -> new pro-U.S. leader
	 - => Increased pressure from North on Cambodia => U.S. sends troops to neutral Cambodia
		 - -> American public outraged => **Kent State University Protest**, 4 students killed confronting National Guard
## Barriers to Success for the U.S.
- **Vietnamization** reduces troops, 1/2 mil. -> 3/4 mil. by 1971
- Low moral, lack of support from military and American public
	- Soldiers want to avoid dangerous missions
	- Black Am. encouraged by Civil Rights movement, didn't see point in fighting "the White Man's war"
## 1972
- North launches massive invasion of South
	- Knew it was a presidential election year, troops were reduced to 95k
- Nixon pushes mass B-52 bombing mission on North capital, Hanoi
	- U.S. drops 36k tons of bombs on Hanoi in 12 days
- Soviet SAMS down 15 B-52's
- 44 pilots captured, 1600 civilians killed
## 1973 - Paris Peace Accords
*January 31, 1973*
- U.S. *withdrew troops*, gained return of P.O.W.'s, left a few advisors behind
- *1973* -> U.S. sends $2.56 bil. in aid to South's govt.
- *1974* -> $907 mil. aid
- *1975* -> $700 mil. aid
## North Vietnam Victory
- South losing badly, 90% inflation, high unemployment
- Congress denied Pres. Ford's request for $400 mil. aid
- North pushed, took 6 Southern provinces, took capital Saigon
	- South *surrendered to the North* on **April 30, 1975**
# Conclusion
- U.S. never lost a *major battle* in Vietnam
- Challenge was who had more will power, + U.S. was supporting an unpopular govt. in South
- North Vietnam + Viet Cong was willing to lose many lives to win
- 2-3 mil. Vietnamese killed
- 58k Americans killed
- Cost $150+ billion
- U.S. used *more than 3 times* the # of bombs used in WWII
