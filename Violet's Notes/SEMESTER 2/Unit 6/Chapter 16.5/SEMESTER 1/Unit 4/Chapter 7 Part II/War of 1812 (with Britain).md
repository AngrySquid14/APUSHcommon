### 1807 Chesapeake-Leopard Incident
- Am. Chesapeake ship sailed from Norfolk, VA
	- Received fire from Br. Ship (Leopold) & took 4 sailors
- Jefferson responds by *expelling British Warships from American waters*
	- Sends Minister James Monroe to Britain to end "impressment" practice
		- Britain offers *compensation for Chesapeake killed & wounded & return 3/4 captives*
		- Doesn't result in end of impressment
### 1811 Battle of Tippecanoe
- William Henry Harrison defeated Tecumseh's alliance w/ 1k soldiers + burned the town
### War of 1812
- *1812* - attacks continue on white settlers, *aided by British agents* and supplies
	- Violation of *Jay's* Treaty?
	- Harrison + others felt only way to stop is to rid Canada of Br. and annex it
- Desire to get Florida from Spain irked Br. and 1810 elections brought in a number of **War Hawks**
	- eager for war w/ Britain (Henry Clay-Kentucky, John C. Calhoun-S.Carolina) pressure increased & **Madison gave into a congress declaration of War** (*June 18, 1812*)
	- Am. Surrender Detroit & Fr. Dearborn (Chicago) in 1st months of war
		- *Frigates* (*Small war ships*) and privateers had early success on seas post war w/ Fr. (1813) 
		- *Br. Navy blockades U.S.*
- Great Lakes - Oliver Perry defeats Br. at Put-in Bay (Sep. 1813), *burned York* April 1813 (Toronto), Harrison wins battle at Thames R. Oct. 1813 (killed Tecumseh)
	- *Diminishes Indian morale*
- *Andrew Jackson* defeats Creeks at Horseshoe Bend (March 1814) moved to Florida and captured Pensacola (November 1814)
- Aug. 1814 British land at Chesapeake and *burn/capture Capital Washington D.C.*
- Am. repel attack at Ft. McHenry Sep. 1814 (Baltimore) 
	- **Francis Scott Key** (Washington lawyer aboard Br. ship trying to regain Am. prisoners watching the battle thru the night) writes *Star Spangled Banner* (set to old English drinking song tune)
		- Declared National anthem in 1931
- Sep. 1814, Stopped a Br. Invasion of N.Y. (battle of Plattsburgh), Andrew Jackson led a lopsided victory of Br. at **New Orleans** Jan. 1815 (*700 dead, 1400 wounded, 500 captured - 21 Am. Casualties*) **Peace treaty was already signed Weeks before.**
### New England Revolt (*End of the Federalist Party*)
- U.S. war and failures led to *increased opposition to war* and Federalist opposition of Republican-led government 
	 - particularly in New England, led by Daniel Webster (sought *a separate nation void of slave holders, and back woodsman*)
- *Hartford Convention (12/15/1814)* to discuss grievances and 7 Constitutional amendments protecting  *New England* with Jackson’s huge victory and *treaty ending* the war 
	- meeting became futile, even treasonous -> served a serious blow to the *Federalist Party*
### Treaty of Ghent
 - 12/24/1814-(Belgium) Treaty w/ Br. did little but end fighting. - Am. Dropped call to end Impressments, and Br. drop call for Indian buffer state in NW territory.
 - Free Trade Agreement 1815, *Rush-Bagot Agreement* 1817- mutual disarmament in Great lakes
- Disastrous for Native Americans as US could continue *to pursue native lands w/o native allies*
## War of 1812 Significance
#### National Identity:
- The war produced iconic symbols like the Star-Spangled Banner, which became the national anthem, and *strengthened American patriotism*
#### Assertion of Sovereignty:
- Ability to stand up to the British, the US demonstrated its *independence and right to* **neutral trade**
#### Military Development:
- Highlighted weaknesses of the American military, leading to improvements and *building up the navy*
#### Impact on Native Americans:
- Contributed to the *declare of Native American resistance to Westward Expansion*
#### Political Shifts:
- The war contributed to the *decline of the Federalist Party* and the rise of a more unified nation *(Era of Good Feelings)*
## War of 1812 Summary
- Embargo Act of 1807 was a bill that barred trade between the United States of America and other nations. It was created at the request of President Thomas Jefferson in an attempt to prevent American involvement in the Napoleonic Wars.
- In 1807 the British warship attacked the U.S. navy vessel Chesapeake.
- The Nonintercourse Act of 1809 provided that Americans could now trade with all nations except Britain and France.
- Macon’s Bill No. 2 (1810) provided that if either Britain or France formally agreed to respect U.S. neutral rights at sea, then the United States would prohibit trade with that nation’s foe.
- **War Hawks were known because of their eagerness for war with Britain.**
- The Battle of Tippecanoe (1811) was fought on November 7, between United States forces led by Governor William Henry Harrison of the Indiana Territory and forces of Tecumseh's growing American Indian confederation led by his brother, Tenskwatawa.
- Battle of New Orleans (1815) was when troops led by Andrew Jackson halted a major British effort to control the Mississippi River. The victory however was meaningless because the battle was fought two weeks after the Treaty of Ghent that ended the war.
