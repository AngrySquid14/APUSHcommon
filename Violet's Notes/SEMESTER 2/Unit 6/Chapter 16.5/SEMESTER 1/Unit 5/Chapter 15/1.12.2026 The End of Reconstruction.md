## Why did Reconstruction End/Fail?
- *The North's waning determination to reconstruct the South*
	- By 1877, economic (*Panic of 1873*) + political issues (*Election of 1876*) => Northerners want to move on from Reconstruction
- **Compromise of 1877**:
	- Hayes (Repub.) wins presidency if military is withdrawn from South
		- North gains Republican President
		- South gets military removed => return to white supremacy societal order
## The Black Vote
### Constitutional Rights *Post Civil War*
- 14th -> Black Am. gain citizenship *and allowed Black voting*
- 15th -> Further protected right to vote & to *not be denied by race, color, or former condition of slavery*
- After Civil War => Congress act to prevent Southerners from re-establishing white supremacy
- *1867* - *Radical Republicans* in Congress impose **federal military rule** over most of South
	- Former Confed. states wrote new constitutions under *U.S. Army occupation*
	- Readmitted to Union only after ratifying the *14th Amendment*
- *Reconstruction Amendment prohibited states from denying equal protection of the laws to U.S. Citizens*
- 1870 - 15th Amendment Ratified
### Election Success
- more than 500k black men become voters in south (1870's)
	- almost all vote for Republican Party
- Some states >1/2 of pop. is former enslaved
- 1870-77, in South, 2 Black Am. represent Mississippi in Senate, + 14 members elected to Congress
	- Many of 14 were former slaves
### Denying the Vote
- **Reconstruction ends in 1877** => Fed. troops leave South
- New State constitutions require *annual poll tax* -> voters pay for 2 yrs. before election
- Most difficult voting barrier was the **Literacy Test**
	- required prospecting voters to be able to read and explain a section of state constitution to county clerk
		- county clerk (almost always white) decide if person was illiterate
	- excluded almost all black men, illiterate or not
		- **Why?** clerk would select complicated passages for blacks, and simple sentences for whites
- <u> <b>As a result, by 1940, only <i>3 percent</i> of voting-age black men & women were registered to vote in the South</b> </u>
