**Key Concept 6.2, II, D** *In an urban atmosphere where the access to power was unequally distributed, **political machines** provided social services in exchange for political support, settlement houses helped immigrants adapt to the new language and customs, and women’s clubs and self-help groups targeted intellectual development and social and political reform.* 
# Urban Machines/Tammany Hall
- ## Development
	- rise of *urban centers* relied on private enterprise to provide city *innovations*—street cars, sewage, electricity, etc.—gaining contracts + retail licenses was through **political machines**
		- local political parties kept hold of influential public offices
			-  *machines* (ex. Tammany Hall) had layers of political power, neighborhood to city leaders
	- political machines provided jobs, contracts, + city services
		- gave poor jobs, repaired homes, general community services -> gain votes and allegiance
	- machine system were corrupt, involving favors, bribes, etc.
- ## Structure of the Political/Urban Machine
	- ***City Boss*** -> **Ward Boss/Ward Heeler** -> *Precinct Captain* -> Immigrants
		- City Boss => control the political party activity throughout the city, control *municipal jobs* (city jobs)
		- Ward Boss => secure votes of ward precinct
		- Precinct Captain => Get city block votes, report to ward boss
		- Immigrants => bottom tier, gain jobs, citizenship, shelter, *in exchange for votes*
			- Immigrants and poor *were actually helped by political machines*
	- **Boss Tweed** -> NY Democrat, Tammany Hall (1869-1871)
		- One of the most corrupt politicians in NYC
			- Used intimidation tactics to keep ballot counts under his control
			- Took control of Democratic party in Tammany Hall organization
			- Pocketed 200 mil. from cities in form of *kickbacks* + *payoffs*
				- Kickbacks = bribery
		- Eventually, Tweed took blame for corruption in NYC
			- stripped of assets, imprisoned (1876), died imprisoned in 1878
