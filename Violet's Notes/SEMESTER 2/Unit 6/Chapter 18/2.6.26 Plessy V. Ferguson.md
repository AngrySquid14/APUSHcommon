# Plessy V. Ferguson (**1896**)
- ## Background
	- State of Louisiana enacts law (*1890*) requiring *segregated railway cars*
	- *1892* -> *Homer Adolph Plessy* (1/8th African American, Creole + Mixed-race) and other members of a local *Civil Rights Committee* want to challenge law
		- Homer selected b/c able to pass as white -> purchases 1st class ticket for "whites only" train car
			- -> Announces he is colored, refuses to move, and is arrested
- ***Legal Question: Is Louisiana's law requiring segregated train cars an <u><b><i>unconstitutional infringement</i></b></u> on BOTH the privileges/immunities AND the equal protection clauses of the 14th Amendment?***
- ## Proceedings
	- Plessy's lawyer argues doctrine "separate but equal" was a *sham*
		- Nobody listens -> **May 18th, 1896**, 8 white men rule against Plessy, *7 to 1* -> Majority opinion *justifies "**Separate But Equal**"* 
			- => Segregation did not in itself constitute *unlawful discrimination*
- ## Outcome
	- Decision allowed segregation by race *to grow in Louisiana and across the South*
