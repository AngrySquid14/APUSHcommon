### Tariffs: A Brief Review
#### What is a tariff?
- Tax on *foreign goods*
	- Raises the price of foreign made products
- In theory, this will benefit American industries as foreign products now cost more
- Who would *favor* tariffs?
	- The North - industry
		- Want to protect industries 
- Who would be *against* tariffs?
	- *South* & West – little industry, <u>consumers</U>
		- farming, raising animals
#### Nullification Crisis
- **Northern Arguments**
	- Tariffs following War of 1812 protect domestic industries
		- Industries mainly in New England
	- Tariffs necessary to keep U.S. industries alive
	- Tariffs necessary to produce war materials for the U.S.'s defense
	- Tariffs will provide significant revenue
- **Southern Arguments**
	- Hated tariffs, made them pay significantly higher prices for manufactured goods vs. imports
	- Decreased money spent on imports from Europe -> Europe has less money to spend on Southern cotton and tobacco
##### Tariff of 1828 (Tariff of *Abominations*)
- Passed in 1828 in hopes to hurt President Adams
	- Hoped he would veto it and damage reputation
- Andrew Jackson elected partially in response to tariff, hopes that he would protect Southern interests
- *Raised tariff rates significantly* (**40-50%**)
- Favored Northern industries, hurt Southerners
	- Southerners received *no tariff protection* for their goods
#### John C. Calhoun
- **Who was he?**
	- Part of “Great Triumvirate”
	- Vice President under Quincy Adams and Jackson
	- Senator from South Carolina
- **The South Carolina Exposition and Protest**
- Written anonymously by Calhoun
- Drew inspiration from *VA and KY resolutions*
	- Encouraged states to nullify the Tariff of Abominations
- **Webster-Hayne Debate (1830):**
- Daniel Webster, support union, critical of secessionists
#### Tariff of 1832
- <u>Lowered rates from the Tariff of Abominations</U>
- However, the South felt they were still too high (50% -> 35%(?))
- SC calls a *state convention*
- Officially vote to nullify (*void*) the Tariff of 1832
- SC also threatened secession if DC *collected tariffs by force*
#### A Hero is Needed (*Henry Clay*)
- Same guy who made 36-30 Line
- The “Great Compromiser’s” 2nd Compromise - After Missouri
- **Compromise Tariff of 1833**
	- Over the next 8 years, the tariff rates would fall by *8% per year*
	- South favored compromise
##### Force Bill:
- Passed same day as Tariff of 1833
- The President could use the *millitary to collect tariffs if necessary*
- Both Jackson and Calhoun saved face
	- Union doesn't get broken, tariffs get lowered
- The crisis is averted (yay)
