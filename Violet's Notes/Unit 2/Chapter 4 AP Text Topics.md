###### The Enlightenment in America *Pages 116-121 7th ed, 127-129 8th ed*
- **What 4 fundamental principles were presented by Enlightened thinkers?**
	- The lawlike order of the natural world
	- The power of human reason
	- The natural rights of individuals
		- includes the right to self-government
	- The progressive improvement of society
- **What views did John Locke have?**
	- *Two Treatises of Government* (1690)
		- advanced revolutionary theory that political authority was not given by God to monarchs (Divine Right of Kings)
		- Believed authority derived from social compacts people made to preserve *natural rights* (rights to life, liberty, and property)
	- Believed people should have power to change government policies, or even the government form itself.
- **What are the viewpoints of a *Deist?***
	- Was a way of thinking, not a religion
	- "My own mind is my own church(TP), I am of a sect by myself(TJ)" -Thomas Paine and Thomas Jefferson, deists
	- Believed that a Supreme Being/Grand Architect created the world, allowing it to operate by natural laws
		- Did NOT believe in God's intervention into the world (Traditional Christian belief)
		- Rejected divinity of Christ and authority of Bible
	- "Natural reason" emphasized
		- Questioned right and wrong, questioned slavery, explored science, etc.
###### American Pietism & the Great Awakening *Pages 117-121 7th ed, 129-132 8th ed*
- **What did Edwards preach?**
	- Pietism, a movement emphasizing being pious, emotional worship, and mystical union with God.
		- Work of God crucial to movement, contrast with Deism
	- Edwards published an account of the revival of Pietism in Massachusetts called *A Faithful Narrative of the Surprising Work of God* (1737)
	- Edwards revival of Pietism in Northampton, MA spread throughout the Connecticut River Valley.
