##### George Greenville: Imperial Reformer
- **Reducing expenses of Empire = Initiate new taxation policies**
- Fully integrate colonial America into Empire
- Impress upon colonists that they have responsibilities to Empire
- Hold line on *mercantilism*
	- Reduce illegal smuggling in colonies
	- Restrict colonial manufacturing development
- English citizens paid *5x* colonists' taxes
- **Imperial means to an end
	- Proclamation of 1763
	- Stamp Act of 1765
	- Sugar Act
	- Currency Act
	- Quartering Act
##### Stamp Act
- *1765, Parliament passes Stamp Act (tax on colonial documents)* + Quartering Act
	- Pays cost of having British troops in colonies
- Sugar Act & Stamp Act violators could be tried in vice-admiralty courts (guilty, have to prove selves innocent)
##### Politician Protest and Crowds Rebel
- Nine colonies sent delegates to a Stamp Act Congress to protest *taxation w/o representation*
- Mobs (Sons of Liberty in Boston) use threats & violence to force tax collectors to quit\
- *Economics, liberty, British corruption, and religion all fueled tax protests*
#### Ideological Roots of Resistance
- Colonial resistance starts in seaports (had trade and population)
- Patriot thinking shaped by lawyers drawing on English common law, Enlightenment thinking, & Whig philosophy
- *Tax Resistance took 3 forms:*
	- Intellectual Protest
	- Economic Boycotts
	- Violent Intimidation
- British lawmaking for colonies based upon theory of *"virtual representation"*, by which the role of Parliament was to legislate on behalf of the Colonists
	- **Colonists vehemently reject this theory as illegitimate!**
##### Colonists' View
- Identified selves as Englishmen, *w/ full possession of all the political rights of Englishmen* who just happened to live in America
- Fell back upon traditions of Salutary Neglect, as well as the democratizing influences of the Great Awakening, and Enlightenment ideals.
##### Parliament Compromises, 1766
- Colonial protests, a boycott of English goods, a divided Parliament, and a new PM led to *repeal of Stamp Act in 1766*
- Parliament pass the Declaratory Act in 1766 to reassert right to tax colonies
- *Taxation, Parliamentary supremacy, and large British war debt remained unsettled issues.*
##### Charles Townshend Steps In
- *Townshend Acts of 1767 tax imports of paper, paint glass, & tea* to pay for British military costs + administrative salaries in America
- **Townshend Acts also provided for writs of assistance** *(general search warrants)*, greatly angered many colonists
- Restraining Act of 1767 suspended New York's legislature and signaled a new attitude towards colonial assemblies
##### America Debates and Resists Again
- Colonists protested new taxes thru *nonimportation agreements*
	- + use of homespun created by *Daughters of Liberty* (homespun fabrics)
- British collected a fraction of what they expected from tax acts
### Key Takeaways
- British expect colonists to pay their fair share thru taxes
	-  Colonists view Parliament's taxes as unfair burden
- Colonists argue a *lack of representation*, dismissing the notion of their interests being *Virtually Represented* 
- ###### No Taxation Without Representation
- A strong resistance movement grew based on:
	- Equal citizens' rights (British)
	- Enlightenment ideas - consent of the governed, etc.
	- Self-rule traditions (Salutary Neglect) *prior to the 7 Years' War*
