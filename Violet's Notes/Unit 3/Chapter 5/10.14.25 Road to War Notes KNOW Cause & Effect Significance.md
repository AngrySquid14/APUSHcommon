### Road to War

#### Loyalists
- Strong, unified empire
- Believes they are British citizens
	- Weak w/o Britain belief
- Colonies profit from trade w/ England
- Colonies *too far from England to have representation in Parliament*, think that's fair
- Fought in British army
	- Worked for the king, benefitted from status quo
#### Patriots
- People have rights government can't take away (property)
- Taxation takes away property
- Colonists don't have voice in Parliament, so they can't tax colonies *(No Taxation Without Representation)*
	- *Colonies could never be represented in Parliament - It's too far*, think that's unfair
- Led by Sons of Liberty/ Continental Congress
	- Lead boycotts, protests
#### Tea Act
- intended to help struggling *British East India Company*
	- allowed them to sell surplus tea directly to American colonies w/o import taxes
	- NOT A TAX ON TEA
- Reduced price of British tea, making it cheaper than (smuggled) Dutch tea
- Colonists saw Tea Act as threat
	- b/c it reinforced Parliament's ability to tax w/o colonies' permission
	- Undercut colonial merchants who sold *smuggled* tea
#### Boston Tea Party **(December 16th, 1773)
- Organized by *Sons of Liberty*
	- colonists boarded 3 British ships in Boston Harbor, dump *342 chests of tea* into harbor
		- Dressed as Mohawk natives???

#### Coercive Acts** **(March 1774)** (Intolerable Acts)- response to Boston Tea Party (1773)
- Repay dumped Tea
- (Port Act)- **Closed Boston Harbor to trade**
	- Crippled Massachusetts because Boston Harbor was huge trade port
- Increased British Troops
	- New Quartering Act to house troops
- Suspend Colonist Town meetings
- Appoint British Military Governor, *General Gage* to Massachusetts
- Allowed British officials to be tried in another colony or in England + royal power to appoint judges and sheriffs *(Administration of Justice Act)*
- *(Quebec Act)* - Extend Quebec border south to Ohio River
	- Made Massachusetts smaller
- **Response to Intolerable Acts**
	- Other counties of Boston sent resources
#### 1st Continental Congress (Fall-1774)
- *Representatives from 12 Colonies met in Philadelphia to respond to **Coercive Acts**
- Patrick Henry (Virginia) - "Give me liberty or give me death"
	- Notion of "American" identity and nation gains more traction
- Meeting established an organized boycott of British goods, *involving commoners*
- Delegates vary on views: End Coercive Acts or Fight for Independence. They do agree on Ideal of Liberty.
- Radicals John Hancock & Samuel Adams formed their “own government” and began to stockpile weapons
#### Minutemen
- In conjunction w/ 1st Continental Congress + rising tensions w/ Great Britain, Massachusetts legislature reorganize militia to form elite volunteer companies to deploy at *a minute's notice* (Hence named Minutemen?)
- "Minute men" more loyal and patriotic
#### Battle of Lexington and Concord (Massachusetts)
##### 4/19/1775
- Gage sends troops to capture Hancock and Adams in Lexington and seize weapons in stockpiled in Concord.
- “The British are coming”- Local Patriot Militia met British at *Lexington*, ordered to disperse, shot fired **(Shot Heard Around the World)**
	- 8 colonists died
- British troops return to Boston and met by Minutemen at Concord
	- Results in 200 British casualties
#### 2nd Continental Congress **(May 1775)**
- Organize for war
- *All 13 colonies* represented in Philadelphia
- Established a Continental Army, commanded by **George Washington**
- Establish local colony committee volunteers
- Not ready to declare independence
- *First continental congress starts boycott, second starts army*
#### July 1775
##### **Olive Branch Petition**- To King George III
- *Most Colonists hoped to remain in the British Empire*, but not pay taxes decided by British Parliament
- Sent the petition stating allegiance to the king, but not parliament.
	- King rejected petition, increased troop numbers in Boston
#### January 1776
##### **Common Sense** by Thomas Paine
- Pamphlet that became a bestseller, convinced 1000s to support revolution
	- *Appealed to common people*
	- Attacked king, used reason + **Enlightenment Ideas** to argue for separation from the Crown
- About 500,000 copies printed (of a population of 2.5 million) helped turn the tide for Revolutionary Fervor.
- Afterwards, colonists *no longer wanted* to stay in British Empire
#### July 4, 1776 - It's Too Late to Apologize
##### **Declaration of Independence**- Meet in Philadelphia, drafted by Thomas Jefferson
- Signed by delegates of the 2nd Continental Congress
-  Stated Grievances and final resolve to be Independent
- Included Enlightenment concepts “endowed by their Creator with certain **unalienable Rights** <span style="color: teal; font-size: 0.8em;">(inherent rights)</span>, that among these are *Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness*”.
